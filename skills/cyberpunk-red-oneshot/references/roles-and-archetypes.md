# Cyberpunk RED — Roles and Archetypes

Cyberpunk RED defines ten player Roles, each with a signature Role Ability.
Five of them are documented on R. Talsorian Games' free Cyberpunk RED Easy
Mode rules on the public Roll20 Compendium and are marked **verified** below,
with the exact source URL. For the other five, no page exists under
`Roles:<RoleName>?expansion=0` as far as could be determined — no dedicated
Role page for any of these five turned up during this task's research, and
Task 5's research separately found no free-source coverage of Netrunning/NET
mechanics generally (via the "Playing the Game" page's own text and the Free
Basic Rules index; see `cpr-rules-summary.md`'s Netrunning section). These
five are marked **unverified**. Their descriptions are a best-effort summary
from general published knowledge of the system, not from a confirmed source,
and must be checked against the core rulebook before being treated as
mechanically exact.

## Verified Roles (Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode, Roll20 Compendium)

### Rockerboy — source: verified (Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode)

Source: <https://roll20.net/compendium/cpunk/Roles:Rockerboy?expansion=0>

**Role Ability: Charismatic Impact**

Charismatic Impact lets a Rockerboy influence others by sheer presence of
personality. The Rockerboy need not be a musical performer — the influence
can come through poetry, art, dance, or plain physical presence. Rockerboys
are the street poets, the social conscience, and the rebels of the setting;
as their Charismatic Impact Rank increases, they can sway progressively
larger groups and call on greater loyalty from their fans and followers.

### Solo — source: verified (Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode)

Source: <https://roll20.net/compendium/cpunk/Roles:Solo?expansion=0>

**Role Ability: Combat Awareness**

With Combat Awareness, a Solo can call up their training to have an enhanced
situational awareness of the battlefield. When Combat begins, anytime outside
of Combat, or in Combat with an Action, a Solo may divide the total number of
points they have in their Combat Awareness Role Ability among a number of
Combat abilities. Point assignments persist until changed. Activating certain
abilities costs more points than others.

### Tech — source: verified (Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode)

Source: <https://roll20.net/compendium/cpunk/Roles:Tech?expansion=0>

**Role Ability: Maker**

Using Maker, a Tech can fix, improve, modify, make, and invent new items.
Techs are addicted to technology and make their living building, fixing, and
modifying equipment in a post-War world with broken supply chains — including
lucrative, enemy-making black market work on illegal weapons and stolen
cybertech. When a Tech increases their Maker Rank, they gain one rank in two
different Maker Specialties of their choice, drawn from: repairing,
upgrading, fabricating, and inventing.

### Medtech — source: verified (Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode)

Source: <https://roll20.net/compendium/cpunk/Roles:Medtech?expansion=0>

**Role Ability: Medicine**

Medicine lets a Medtech keep people alive through knowledge, tools, and
training. Medtechs are hybrid doctors and mechanics for a world where people
are often more machine than human — to a Medtech, the human body is their
canvas. When increasing their Medicine Rank, a Medtech allocates points to
one of three specialties: surgery, pharmaceuticals, or cryosystems operation.

### Media — source: verified (Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode)

Source: <https://roll20.net/compendium/cpunk/Roles:Media?expansion=0>

**Role Ability: Credibility**

Credibility lets a Media not only convince an audience of the truth of what
they publish, but also grants a larger audience the more credible they
become. Medias are independent journalists who expose corporate corruption
and government wrongdoing, rather than working for a major media corporation;
their extensive connections and rising credibility also mean they gain
greater access to sources and information, and passively pick up on rumors
and leads through their network.

## Unverified Roles (no free-source page found)

The remaining five Roles have no page under
`https://roll20.net/compendium/cpunk/Roles:<RoleName>?expansion=0` in the
free Cyberpunk RED Easy Mode compendium, as far as could be determined — no
dedicated Role page for any of these five was found during this task's
research. Task 5's research found no free-source coverage of Netrunning/NET
mechanics generally (via the "Playing the Game" page's own text and the Free
Basic Rules index not linking a Netrunning page), which independently
supports the same conclusion for Netrunner specifically. The descriptions
below are a best-effort summary from general published knowledge of
Cyberpunk RED, not sourced from any free reference.

### Netrunner — source: unverified, no free reference found

**Role Ability: Interface (best-effort description)**

The Netrunner's Role Ability, Interface, governs their skill operating in
NET architecture (cyberspace): running programs, executing actions against
NET architecture defenses, and controlling their avatar's speed and
capability inside the Net. Interface Rank is generally understood to gate
both a Netrunner's actions-per-turn in NET space and the strength of
programs they can effectively run. This overlaps directly with the
Netrunning / NET architecture rules gap already flagged in
`cpr-rules-summary.md` — none of that subsystem is confirmed from a free
source. GM should confirm exact mechanics against the core rulebook before
play.

### Exec — source: unverified, no free reference found

**Role Ability: Teamwork (best-effort description)**

The Exec's Role Ability, Teamwork, is generally understood to reflect their
position commanding corporate resources — staff, assets, and favors — and
their ability to direct a team toward a shared objective, granting bonuses
to allies acting under their direction or coordination. Execs are corporate
managers and power players who leverage organizational assets rather than
personal combat or technical skill. GM should confirm exact mechanics
against the core rulebook before play.

### Fixer — source: unverified, no free reference found

**Role Ability: Operator (best-effort description)**

The Fixer's Role Ability, Operator, is generally understood to represent
their web of contacts and deals — the ability to broker the acquisition of
goods, services, and information (including illegal or hard-to-find items)
faster or cheaper than normal, and to call in favors from their network.
Fixers are the deal-makers and information brokers of Night City. GM should
confirm exact mechanics against the core rulebook before play.

### Lawman — source: unverified, no free reference found

**Role Ability: Backup (best-effort description)**

The Lawman's Role Ability, Backup, is generally understood to let them call
on fellow law enforcement for assistance — reinforcements, information, or
favors — reflecting their standing within a police force or similar
organization. The scale and reliability of the Backup they can summon is
generally understood to scale with their Role Ability Rank. GM should
confirm exact mechanics against the core rulebook before play.

### Nomad — source: unverified, no free reference found

**Role Ability: Moto (best-effort description)**

The Nomad's Role Ability, Moto, is generally understood to cover their skill
with vehicles and their standing within their Nomad family/pack — including
bonuses to vehicle-related actions and the ability to call on family members
for aid, gear, or safe haven. Nomads are family- and clan-oriented wanderers
of the Combat Zone and Badlands. GM should confirm exact mechanics against
the core rulebook before play.

## Balanced Party Selection

When auto-picking Roles for a 3-5 player one-shot party, follow this simple
rule to keep the party functional without overloading any single mechanic
gap:

1. **Always include at least one combat-capable Role** — Solo, Nomad, or
   Lawman — so the party can survive a fight.
2. **Always include at least one tech/support Role** — Tech, Medtech, or
   Netrunner — so the party can patch up wounds, fix or build gear, or
   handle technical obstacles.
3. **Fill remaining slots for social/utility coverage** based on the GM's
   premise — Fixer, Media, Rockerboy, or Exec — to round out contacts,
   persuasion, and information-gathering.

**Netrunner caveat:** only select Netrunner to fill the tech/support slot if
the GM's premise explicitly calls for hacking or NET-based scenes. Cyberpunk
RED's Netrunning / NET architecture rules are not covered by the free Easy
Mode source (see `cpr-rules-summary.md`), so running a Netrunner PC means
improvising or confirming mechanics against the core rulebook mid-session.
For premises that don't specifically need hacking, prefer Tech or Medtech
for the tech/support slot instead.
